# Arithmetic & Elementary Algebra

Part of [[Curriculum Connections]]. Where the arithmetic and pre-algebra your class is working on shows up in the exhibit — with a paragraph you can forward to justify the trip, and a few things to point out once you arrive.

## Why this trip counts

Mathemalchemy turns core arithmetic and pre-algebra ideas into things students can see rendered in physical, artistic form — not just on a printed page or a computer screen: **place value and number bases**, **prime numbers and factoring**, **remainders and modular ("clock") arithmetic**, and the leap from a repeating pattern to a **general rule** — the move that underlies all of algebra. A visit reinforces number sense and shows students that the arithmetic they practice by hand is the actual machinery behind real secret codes, art, and biology. No special preparation is needed — but here are a few highlights to look for when you arrive, or to review after visiting.

## Where your topics show up

| Concept | Scene | What to point out |
|---|---|---|
| **Place value & number bases** | [[Cryptography Quilt]] · [[Silhouettes and Vortices]] | The quilt's padlock spells a word in **8-bit binary** (base 2); the ring of squares around it counts in **base 3**, using three shades of blue for the digits 0, 1, 2. Same idea as our base-10 place value — different base. The Silhouettes banner hides a binary message too. |
| **Primes & factoring — with & without remainders** | [[The Garden and Reef]] | In the **garden**, squirrels run a **Sieve of Eratosthenes** (they keep 2, 3, 5, 7, 11), and chipmunks play a **prime-vs-composite** game with cuneiform number tiles and acorns. Underneath it is division **with and without a remainder**: whether a number divides evenly is exactly the test for a factor (the braided rings even warn "don't eat the remainders"). |
| **Remainders & modular arithmetic** | [[Cryptography Quilt]] | A **Caesar cipher** just shifts each letter and wraps around the alphabet — that "wrap-around" is clock arithmetic. The garden's braided rings even joke about "not eating the remainders." |
| **Patterns into rules** | [[Frieze Tables]] · [[Quiltlet]] | There are **exactly 7** border (frieze) patterns and **exactly 17** wallpaper patterns — no more. Going from "I see it repeat" to "there are exactly *N* of them" is the generalizing move behind variables and formulas. |
| **Algebraic equations & formulas** | [[The Curio Shop]] | Euler's polyhedron formula **V − E + F = 2** ties together a solid's corners, edges, and faces. Count any two on a real model and you can **solve for the third** — the "find the unknown" move of beginning algebra, made concrete on a 3-D object. |
| **Equations and their graphs** | [[Cryptography Quilt]] | Block D shows an **elliptic curve, y² = x³ + a x + b** — an equation in *x* and *y*. No need to solve it: the point students *can* see is that two variables in one equation trace a **curve in the plane**, and that curve is the engine behind elliptic-curve cryptography. |

## Ready-made activities

From the [[Activities Index]]:
- **[[Decode the Banner]]** — turn a binary pattern into a number by hand (powers of two; place value in action), then into a letter.
- **[[Decode the Padlock]]** — the same trick on the quilt's padlock; 3–5 minutes in the gallery.
- **[[Decode the Ring]]** — build a base-3 alphabet, then crack the ternary message circling the padlock (classroom, 20–30 min).
- **[[Cipher Safari]]** — name the real cipher hidden in each of the quilt's 20 blocks.

## But let them wander

This is a map of where *your* topics live, not a route to march through. Point out one or two of these, then set students loose — the prime-sorting chipmunks and the secret codes tend to pull kids in on their own, and the best moments are the ones they find for themselves.
