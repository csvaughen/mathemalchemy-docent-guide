# Stack of Books

**Math:** [[Mathematical History and the Long Conversation]] · [[Women in Mathematics]]
**Official page:** https://mathemalchemy.org/2022/01/11/math-connections-in-the-stack-of-books/

## What it is
A stack of books, with origami flowers — but not a random pile of old volumes. The stack is built in four deliberate layers: **historical roots**, **contemporary mathematics done seriously**, **contemporary mathematics done playfully**, and **books that aren't about mathematics at all** but are full of it anyway.

The detail nearly everyone walks past: **every book has a bookmark, and the bookmark is a portrait.** The face tucked into each volume belongs to someone the book connects to — usually someone who came along much later and was still arguing with it. Euclid is bookmarked by the two men who broke his fifth postulate two thousand years afterward. Coxeter is bookmarked by Escher, who really did write to him. The books are the works; the bookmarks are who was still talking.

## Docent questions
- **What's the oldest thing in this pile — and is any of it wrong yet?** (Nothing in the historical layer has been retracted. Euclid's theorems are still true. That is not how most 2,300-year-old books have aged.)
- **Open any book in your mind — why is there a *face* in it?** Every bookmark is a portrait of someone the book connects to. Pick one and work out the link.
- **Two of these aren't mathematics books. Can you spot them, and guess why they made the stack?** (Weaving and ocean navigation — both are mathematics that nobody filed under mathematics.)
