# The Garden and Reef

**Math:** [[Hyperbolic Geometry]] · [[Fractals and Self-Similarity]] · [[Prime Numbers]] · [[Polyhedra and Euler's Formula]] (100+ 3D-printed solids as flowers and sea creatures)
**Official page:** https://mathemalchemy.org/2022/01/11/math-connections-in-the-garden/

## What it is
A garden and coral reef of **hyperbolic crochet** and folded paper — surfaces that ruffle and curve because they carry more area than flat space allows. Daina Taimina literally invented hyperbolic crochet, and this scene descends from that tradition. 

- **Squirrels** in the *garden* run their annual "Sieving of the Primes" in front of a **Sieve of Eratosthenes** (they store sieves 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 "behind the Riemann Cliffs").
- **Chipmunks** (Uinta and Durango — both chipmunk-species names) play a prime-vs-composite game on the adjacent *playground*, with **Babylonian cuneiform numeral tiles and acorns** ("the braided rings remind us not to eat the remainders").

**The squirrels are hauling in a sieve for 11, and arguing about whether they need it. Their table stops at 100. Who's right?** 

## Key vocabulary
- **Hyperbolic geometry** — space that curves away from itself like a kale leaf; parallel lines *diverge*.
- **Fractal growth** — small parts resembling the whole.
- **Non-Euclidean geometry** — geometry on a surface that isn't flat. There are two kinds, and the giveaway is a triangle: on a sphere its angles add up to more than 180°, on a hyperbolic surface to less. Everything that ruffles here is the hyperbolic kind. The green-and-yellow checkered fabric behind the chipmunks shows why it cannot lie flat — four regular pentagons meet at each corner, and their angles come to about 514° where a flat surface has room for only 360°.
- **Polyhedron** — a solid whose faces are all flat with straight edges. The ones here are **convex**, meaning a line drawn between any two points of the solid stays inside it. They come in four families: the 5 **Platonic** solids (every face the same regular polygon), the 13 **Archimedean**, the 92 **Johnson**, and prisms and antiprisms. You can tell which family you are looking at, because they were made differently on purpose — Platonic solids have every face filled in, Archimedean solids only some, and Johnson solids show their edges alone.
- **Origami** — folded paper. **Traditional** origami works one sheet, as the roses do. **Modular** origami folds many sheets into identical units and assembles them into one symmetric object, which is where most of the reef's shells and flowers come from.
- **Alexander's horned sphere** — the metal sculpture in the garden, and a famous counterexample from the 1920s. It is a closed surface whose *inside* can be reshaped into the inside of an ordinary ball, while its *outside* cannot be reshaped into the outside of one. Mathematicians had assumed that was impossible, and it is the kind of object that exists to show an assumption was wrong.
