# The Curio Shop

**Math:** [[Polyhedra and Euler's Formula]] · mathematical artifacts
**Official page:** https://mathemalchemy.org/2022/01/11/math-connections-in-the-curio-shop/

## What it is
**Conway's Curios**, Harriet the bowerbird's shop of *objets de mathématique* — a cabinet of famous mathematical shapes made physical. Treat it as a **collection, not a checklist**: which gems are out, and where they sit, changes from one installation to the next as the exhibit travels. Look at what's actually on the shelves rather than hunting for a specific piece.

Regulars include ceramic and metal **Klein bottles** and a ceramic **Möbius band**, an **Alexander horned sphere** frozen a few iterations in, the **Conway knot**, **Borromean rings**, halved and linked **tori**, a ruled surface, a beaded **DNA double helix**, and an **iterated-function-system fractal tree**. Expect polyhedra from both big families — **Johnson solids** and **Archimedean solids** such as a beaded **rhombicosidodecahedron** or an origami **snub dodecahedron**.

**Look for the Antikythera mechanism** — depending on the install it may be here in the shop or over in [[Knotical (Knotilus Bay)]].

Fixed features of the scene: the **Harriss spiral** runs through it — shop sign, wall, and Harriet's own feathers — the surrounding pavement is tiled in **Voronoi cells**, and the 17 **wallpaper groups** are worked into embroidery and quilting.

## Lore (AMS Notices, Aug 2022)
- The proprietor is **Harriet, a bowerbird** who "channeled her propensity for collecting mathematically intriguing objects into a thriving business." She's named for the **Harriss spiral** (discovered by team member Edmund Harriss) — the spiral adorns her feathers and the shop sign and wall.
- The shop is named after **John H. Conway, with his family's permission** — like Conway's office, it's full of mathematical curiosities. Harriet's brother **Horton** (Conway's middle name) runs the **terrace café** next door, reached via the lighthouse ramp.
- Inventory called out in the article: Klein bottles, interlocking sliced tori, rhombicosidodecahedra, double helices, fractal trees, Borromean rings — "*objets de mathématique*."
- **Window display:** an **Alexander horned sphere** hung next to the **Möbius strip**, opposite the **Hopf fibration of the three-sphere**. 

## Key vocabulary

- **[Klein bottle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein_bottle)** — a closed surface with only one side; it can't be built in 3D without passing through itself.
- **[Möbius band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6bius_strip)** — a strip given a half-twist and joined: one side, one edge.
- **[Alexander horned sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_horned_sphere)** — a sphere deformed by infinitely many interlocking horns; topologically still a sphere, but its outside is tangled. The shop's version stops after finitely many iterations.
- **[Conway knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_knot)** — an 11-crossing knot whose "sliceness" stayed open ~50 years until **[Lisa Piccirillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Piccirillo)** settled it: topologically slice, not smoothly slice.
- **[Borromean rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borromean_rings)** — three rings linked so that removing any one frees the other two.
- **[Torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torus)** — the doughnut surface; here sliced and re-linked.
- **[Ruled surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruled_surface)** — a curved surface swept out by a straight line, so it can be built from straight rods.
- **[Johnson solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_solid)** — a convex solid whose faces are all regular polygons, but which isn't one of the highly symmetric families; exactly 92 exist.
- **[Archimedean solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedean_solid)** — a convex solid built from two or more kinds of regular polygon, with every corner identical; exactly 13 exist. The dividing line from a Johnson solid is the corners: identical on an Archimedean, not on a Johnson.
- **[Rhombicosidodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombicosidodecahedron)** — an Archimedean solid: 62 faces of squares, triangles, and pentagons.
- **[Snub dodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snub_dodecahedron)** — another Archimedean solid, 92 faces, and one of two that come in left- and right-handed forms.
- **[Antikythera mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antikythera_mechanism)** — a ~2,000-year-old Greek geared device that predicted astronomical positions and eclipses; the oldest known analog computer. May be in the shop or in the bay, depending on the install.
- **[Golden spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_spiral)** — widens by the golden ratio φ = (1 + √5)/2 every quarter turn; built by splitting a rectangle into a square plus a similar rectangle.
- **Harriss spiral** — built the same way but splitting a rectangle into *two* similar rectangles plus a square, which gives the **[plastic ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_ratio)** ρ ≈ 1.3247 instead of φ. Discovered by team member **[Edmund Harriss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Harriss)**; no Wikipedia article of its own.
- **[Hopf fibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_fibration)** — a way of filling the 3-sphere with circles, every pair of them linked.
- **[Voronoi cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_diagram)** — carve a region into "nearest neighbor" territories, one per seed point; the pavement tiling.
- **[Wallpaper groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallpaper_group)** — the 17 ways a pattern can repeat across a plane (compare the 7 [[Frieze Groups]] for a strip).
- **[Iterated function system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterated_function_system)** — a fractal recipe: apply a small set of shrinking transformations over and over; the shop's fractal tree.
