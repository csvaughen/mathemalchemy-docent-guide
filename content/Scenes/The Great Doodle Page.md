# The Great Doodle Page

**Math:** [[Women in Mathematics]] · [[Mathematical History and the Long Conversation]]
**Official page:** https://mathemalchemy.org/2022/01/11/math-connections-in-the-great-doodle-page/

## What it is
A quilted page of **sketches and doodles** — not finished diagrams — celebrating seven women mathematicians from different fields and different centuries, each one shown through her own hand-drawn working. Lovelace's marginal sketches, Kovalevskaya's flowers doodled over a scratched-out calculation, Mirzakhani's manuscript notes. The argument it makes: this messy stage *is* the mathematics, not the part you clean up before showing anyone.

**Where to find it:** on the **back** of the quilt, partly obscured — it is genuinely easy to walk past, so go around and look.

## Who's on it

| Where | Who | What you're looking at |
|---|---|---|
| Border | **Marjorie Rice** | Her pentagonal tiling of the plane — and some pentagon pairs have detached themselves and are wandering off, trying other arrangements. The top-left pentagons are marked "MR" |
| Top left | **Ada Lovelace** | Sketches from Folio 177 of the Lovelace-Byron papers at Oxford's Bodleian Library. Ursula Martin identified them as work on the Königsberg bridges problem — graph theory before graph theory |
| Top right | **Gladys West** | Two drawings from her papers, on the mathematics of a geostationary satellite network — the modeling that made GPS possible |
| Bottom middle | **Alicia Boole Stott** | Doodles in four-dimensional geometry. She constructed all six regular 4-polytopes, the 4D equivalents of the Platonic solids |
| Bottom middle | **Maryam Mirzakhani** | Manuscript notes from the first woman to win the Fields Medal (2014) |
| Bottom right | **Sofya Kovalevskaya** | Flowers and bugs she doodled over a scratched-out part of a calculation. First woman to earn a math PhD and the first to be a professor |
| Middle right | **Caroline Series** | Kleinian-group drawings, contributed by Series herself |

Full biographies and the Fields Medal thread are in [[Women in Mathematics]].

## Easter egg
The quilt's fabric colors reuse the [[Cryptography Quilt]]'s color-to-letter correspondence: **each mathematician's region is colored by her initials.** A lovely cross-scene decode for anyone who has already done the ternary worksheet — see [[Decode the Ring]].

## Docent questions
- **These are doodles, not diagrams. Why would anyone quilt someone's rough working?** (Because the rough working is what doing mathematics actually looks like — and almost nobody is ever shown it.)
- **One of these seven had no mathematical training at all past high school. Which one, do you think?** (Marjorie Rice, on the border — she learned from a magazine column and found tilings the professionals had missed.)
- **The colors aren't decorative. Anything look familiar?** (Same color code as the Cryptography Quilt; each region spells its mathematician's initials.)
