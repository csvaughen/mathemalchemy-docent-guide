# Cryptography Quilt

**Math:** [[Cryptography]]

**Official page:** https://mathemalchemy.org/2022/01/23/mathematical-connections-in-the-cryptography-quilt/

## What it is
A quilt of **20 labeled blocks (A–T)**, arranged clockwise in four tiers of increasing complexity — basic symbols, historical ciphers, complex ciphers, modern mathematical cryptography — around a central padlock and its ring of five vignettes. Secrets hidden in plain sight through pattern, and genuinely decodable, not decorative: the padlock itself carries an 8-bit **binary/ASCII** message, and the ring of colored squares circling it carries a second message in **ternary (base-3)**, using three shades of blue for the digits 0, 1, and 2. The official *Decoding the Ternary Message* worksheet walks through building a full A–Z decoder dictionary for the ternary one.

## The five vignettes
The quilt is built in rings. The padlock sits at the center; around it is a ring of **five more quilted panels, which the creators call vignettes**; around those are the twenty lettered blocks; and the binding runs around the outside. That makes **twenty-five quilted panels in all** — the five vignettes carry no letters, so a count of the panels and a count of the blocks give different numbers on purpose.

Each vignette ties a different scene of the installation back to the theme of encoding a message.

- **Bakery** — a recipe for mandelbrot, which literally means "almond bread," given as a sequence of pictures instead of written instructions. → [[Mandelbrot Bakery]]
- **Garden** — public-key cryptography, and RSA in particular. A squirrel and a chipmunk exchange speech bubbles that anyone is free to overhear; the shaded items are the private information, and it is knowing the factorization of *n* into primes that lets the chipmunk read what was sent. The message itself is written in the language of flowers. Worth pairing with the animals' own scene, where those same squirrels work a Sieve of Eratosthenes and the chipmunks play prime-versus-composite — the quilt shows them using as a cipher the mathematics they play with at home. → [[The Garden and Reef]], [[Prime Numbers]]
- **Knotical** — a sailor signaling with semaphore flags, spelling the letter **S**, for sailor and for semaphore. Semaphore carried messages between ships throughout the 19th century. → [[Knotical (Knotilus Bay)]]
- **Lighthouse** — the lighthouse flashing a message in Morse code. The creators pose it as a puzzle on their own page: it refers to light and to mathematics, and they ask whether you can tell what it says. → [[The Lighthouse]]
- **Tess** — a kite with a key hanging from it. The key opens the padlock at the center of this quilt, and it is also Benjamin Franklin's kite-and-key experiment; Tess's was the first scene developed for Mathemalchemy, and the creators cast that spark as what set the rest of the installation going. → [[Tess the Tortoise]]

## The binding is the key
Don't skip the edge. The band of fabrics running around the quilt's **binding** is the code's alphabet: **26 fabrics, A through Z, in order.** It's the key to the whole installation's fabric code — including the initials hidden in the colors of [[The Great Doodle Page]], which hangs on the back of this same quilt. Visitors can decode with nothing but the object in front of them.

## The panel attached to it
A small quilted panel on this quilt diagrams **BB84 quantum key distribution** — fireflies as single-photon sources, calcite crystals as polarizing filters, frogs as detectors. Keep the two straight: that panel *uses* quantum physics, while this quilt's lattice block (**Block H**) is classical math built to *survive* quantum computers. → [[Quiltlet]]

## Docent questions
- "Every block on this quilt is a real code or cipher — and you used several of them today without noticing. Pick one: where do you think it touches your everyday life?"
- "When you tap your phone at a SEPTA gate, it makes a code that has never existed before and will never be accepted again. Why go to all that trouble, instead of just handing over your card number?"
- "You tap to buy a coffee. Your fingerprint never leaves your phone, and the shop never learns your real card number — two different secrets, both protected in about a second. Which blocks up there are doing that work?"

## Key vocabulary
- **Cryptography** — protecting information by transforming it so only the intended recipient can undo it.
- **Cipher** — the specific algorithm used to encrypt.
- **Post-quantum cryptography** — classical math designed to stay secure even against a future quantum computer. The quilt's lattice-based block (H) is one of the leading candidates.

## Hidden messages
The padlock and the ring of colored squares around it both carry genuine hidden messages — not decoration. The padlock hides an **8-bit binary/ASCII** message, the same code as the [[Silhouettes and Vortices]] banner; the ring hides a second message in **ternary (base 3)**, using three shades of blue for its digits. There *are* real messages — challenge visitors to crack them, and don't give it away. Full activities: [[Decode the Padlock]] and [[Decode the Ring]].

## Block identification
All 20 blocks (A–T) depict a real cipher or cryptographic idea, arranged in four tiers from basic symbols to modern mathematical cryptography — a natural first-five-minutes activity for a group walking in. Full block-by-block answer key: [[Cipher Safari]].

## Tour tip
Start with "what do you see?" and let the looking happen before any explaining. A good way in for a group: assign pairs to a block and let them puzzle out what it depicts.
