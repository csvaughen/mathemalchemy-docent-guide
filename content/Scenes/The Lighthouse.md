# The Lighthouse

**Math:** [[Polyhedra and Euler's Formula]] (the dodecahedron) · [[Projective Geometry and Perspective]] (projection) · differential geometry (see construction notes)

**Official page:** https://mathemalchemy.org/2022/01/11/math-connections-lighthouse/

## What it is
A nine-foot vase-shaped tower, narrow at the waist, wrapped in a spiral ramp of welded metal strips. Its organizing shape is the **regular heptagon** — the smallest regular polygon that *cannot* be drawn with straightedge and compass. At the top, a **Fresnel lens** and a **dodecahedron** house the light, and a carved **sphere** throws its pattern onto the ceiling: light cast through a sphere onto a flat surface is **stereographic projection**. It distorts sizes badly but gets every angle exactly right — it is **conformal**, the same property that makes the Mercator world map useful for steering a ship.

## Docent questions
- "This whole tower is organized around the seven-sided polygon — the smallest one the Greeks could *not* draw with just a compass and straightedge. Why build with the shape the classical tools can't reach?"
- "Look at the pattern on the ceiling. It's stretched out of shape compared to the sphere it came from — but every angle in it is still exactly right. Where have you seen a map that does that?" (Every Mercator classroom map: Greenland looks continent-sized, but the angles a ship steers by are true. Same *property* — both are conformal — but built differently; Mercator isn't made by shining a light.)
- "Walk in a straight line from any corner of a cube and you'll always run into another corner. On the dodecahedron up there — alone among the five Platonic solids — you can walk from a corner and return to that same corner while missing every other one. Nobody proved that until 2020."

## Key vocabulary
Each term links to its Wikipedia article.

- **[Heptagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heptagon)** — seven-sided polygon; the tower's organizing shape.
- **[Constructible polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructible_polygon)** — one you can draw with straightedge and compass alone. The regular heptagon is the smallest that isn't; which ones are is settled by the Gauss–Wantzel theorem.
- **[Stereographic projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereographic_projection)** — flattening a sphere onto a plane by casting light through it from a single point.
- **[Conformal map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_map)** — a transformation that preserves angles but not lengths. Stereographic projection is one.
- **[Mercator projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercator_projection)** — the conformal world map; true angles at the cost of wildly distorted areas. Conformal like stereographic projection, but *not* the same map and not made by casting light — its north–south stretch has to be computed. (The lamp-inside-a-globe picture people often reach for gives the **central cylindrical** projection, which is not conformal and is not Mercator.)
- **[Fresnel lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnel_lens)** — a lens cut into concentric rings so it bends light like a thick lens with almost none of the glass. Devised by **[Augustin-Jean Fresnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin-Jean_Fresnel)**; the official page calls it "the invention that saved a million ships."
- **[Regular dodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_dodecahedron)** — 12 pentagonal faces, 5-fold symmetry; one of the five **[Platonic solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_solid)**.
- **[Geodesic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic)** — the straightest possible path along a surface.
- **[Hyperboloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperboloid)** of one sheet — the saddle-waisted shape behind the tower's silhouette; made of straight lines despite curving.
- **[Principal curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_curvature)** — the directions in which a surface bends most and least; the ramp follows one of them.
- **[Darboux frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darboux_frame)** — the moving set of axes used to track a curve on a surface; it has no torsion along a line of principal curvature, which is why the metal strips bend without twisting.

## Construction & lore (AMS Notices, Aug 2022)
- The shape is a **perturbed hyperboloid of one sheet**, chosen so one **line of principal curvature** forms the gentle spiral ramp. It's built from welded metal strips with T- or L-shaped cross-sections laid along lines of principal curvature — the **Darboux frame** has no torsion there, so the strips bend without twisting. This math was developed *for* the build — "mathematics developed specifically in response to the challenges of constructing our vision."
- **Nine feet tall**; a **Fresnel lens** projects the beam through a **stained-glass dodecahedron**. Footnote gem: unlike the other four Platonic solids, the dodecahedron admits geodesic paths from a vertex back to itself that avoid every other vertex (published May 2020).
- Tended by two **puffins named Del and Nabla** (∇) — who, in the AMS dialogue, coach Arnold on the engineering of free-standing phyllo towers ("the long straight girders 'round axis Z are shaped like Ts from sky to sea"). An **octopus paints graffiti** beneath the lighthouse.
