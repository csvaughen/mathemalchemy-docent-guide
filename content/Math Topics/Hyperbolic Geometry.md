# Hyperbolic Geometry

**In the exhibit:** [[The Garden and Reef]] (crochet corals, kale-like ruffles)

The non-Euclidean geometry of **constant negative curvature**: every point is shaped like a saddle, lines that never meet spread farther and farther apart, and triangles have angle sums below 180°. There's more room than in a flat plane: a circle's circumference grows exponentially as its radius grows. That extra room can't be laid flat, and it can't be fit smoothly into our 3D space, which is why crocheted hyperbolic planes ruffle. **Daina Taimina** (team member!) invented hyperbolic crochet at Cornell in 1997, giving mathematicians a durable model they could handle, fold, and extend, replacing fragile taped-paper versions.

Nature makes hyperbolic-like ruffles constantly: corals, kelp, kale, sea slugs, all packing lots of surface area into limited space.

**One-liner for tours:** "That ruffle is the shape of a world where lines that never meet still drift apart."
