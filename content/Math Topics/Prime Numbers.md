# Prime Numbers

**In the exhibit:** [[The Garden and Reef]] — squirrels running the Sieve of Eratosthenes, and chipmunks sorting primes, in the garden

Primes are the atoms of arithmetic: every integer > 1 factors uniquely into primes (Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic). Euclid proved there are infinitely many, ~300 BCE; their distribution still holds the field's deepest open problem (the Riemann Hypothesis).

Primes also guard your data: public-key [[Cryptography]] rests on the difficulty of factoring huge numbers into their prime parts.

The garden stages the **Sieve of Eratosthenes**, the oldest prime-finding recipe: keep 2 and cross out its larger multiples, keep 3 and cross out its multiples, keep 5, and so on — whatever survives is prime. The squirrels work it as a set of physical screens laid over the numbers **1 to 100**: the screens for **2, 3, and 5** are already in place, and more squirrels are hauling the **7 and 11** screens toward the table. As they arrive, they're debating one thing — **whether the screen for 11 is needed at all** for a table that stops at 100. Worth pausing on before you move along; left here as an invitation to consider, not to answer.

**One-liner for tours:** "The chipmunks are sorting the atoms of arithmetic — the same numbers that lock your bank account."
