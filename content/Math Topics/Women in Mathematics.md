# Women in Mathematics

**In the exhibit:** [[The Great Doodle Page]] (seven of them, in their own handwriting) · [[Stack of Books]] (the portrait bookmarks) · [[The Garden and Reef]] (Daina Taimina's hyperbolic crochet) · and the team that built the whole thing

This isn't a theme laid over the installation from outside. **Twenty of Mathemalchemy's twenty-four makers are women**, and the project was co-founded by two of them: mathematician **[Ingrid Daubechies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrid_Daubechies)**, whose wavelets underlie modern image compression, and fiber artist **Dominique Ehrmann**, the lead artist and fabricator. The exhibit celebrates women mathematicians because it largely *is* women mathematicians — see [[The Team]].

## Their own handwriting

What makes [[The Great Doodle Page]] unusual is that it doesn't show portraits or achievements. It shows **working**. Lovelace's marginal sketches, Kovalevskaya's flowers doodled over a failed calculation, Mirzakhani's manuscript notes, Gladys West's technical drawings — the actual paper, mid-thought. Not seven women who *were* mathematicians, but seven people caught in the act of doing mathematics, which looks messy for everyone.

**Easter egg:** each mathematician's region uses the [[Cryptography Quilt]]'s color-to-letter code, so the fabric colors spell out her initials.

## Three medals

The Fields Medal has been awarded since 1936. Three women have received it:

- **Maryam Mirzakhani**, 2014 — the first, whose handwriting is on the Doodle Page.
- **[Maryna Viazovska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryna_Viazovska)**, 2022 — sphere packing in dimensions 8 and 24.
- **[Hong Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Wang)**, 2026 — for proving the three-dimensional [Kakeya set conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakeya_set) with Joshua Zahl, a question open for a century: how small can a set be and still contain a needle pointing in every direction?

The third medal was awarded on **23 July 2026, in Philadelphia** — the opening day of the International Congress of Mathematicians that Mathemalchemy had traveled to. A visitor standing at the Doodle Page that week was looking at the notes of the first woman to win it, in the city where the third one was collecting hers.

That is the point of the whole scene, and it isn't history. It's a conversation with open seats.
