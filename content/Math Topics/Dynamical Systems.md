# Dynamical Systems

**In the exhibit:** [[The Terrace]] (Baker's map, traveling waves, Poincaré's telescope) · [[Silhouettes and Vortices]] (an image of Arnold's cat map at the adult silhouette's feet) · echoed in [[Mandelbrot Bakery]] (Arnold the baker cat, the KAM theorem)

A **dynamical system** is just a rule for how a state changes step by step, or moment by moment — and the same handful of rules show up everywhere from kneading dough to predicting orbits. The **[Baker's map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker%27s_map)** stretches a shape and folds it back on itself, exactly like kneading dough — stretch, cut, stack, repeat. Iterate it and two points that started a hair apart end up scattered across the whole square: an early, hands-on picture of **deterministic chaos** — simple, fully determined rules whose outcomes are nonetheless unpredictable. That same stretch-and-fold is also how **mixing** and **diffusion** work — how a swirl of cream spreads through coffee, or two colors of dough blend into one. **[Arnold's cat map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold%27s_cat_map)** makes the mixing visible: it stretches and shears a picture of a cat across a square and wraps the overflow back around, and within a few steps the cat dissolves into evenly-scrambled noise. (Keep iterating on a pixel grid, though, and it eventually snaps back to the original cat — a discrete echo of Poincaré recurrence.) **Henri Poincaré**, working on the three-body problem in celestial mechanics in the 1890s, was the first to show that even simple, fully determined systems can behave this unpredictably — the discovery that founded the field.

**One-liner for tours:** "Simple rules, followed exactly, can still surprise you — that's chaos."
